# LimbX

### Closing the loop between movement and touch.

LimbX is an adaptive, tendon-driven robotic limb designed to bridge the sensory gap in prosthetic and wearable robotic systems.

It uses tactile/force sensors to detect contact and grip, then translates this information into intuitive vibrotactile feedback through an adjustable wearable interface.

---

## The Problem

Prosthetic and wearable robotic hands can move and grasp, but users often cannot feel what the hand is touching.

This lack of real-time touch and force feedback limits control and precision.

LimbX aims to bridge this gap by combining tactile sensing, vibrotactile feedback and an adaptive wearable interface.

---

## Our Solution

LimbX follows a closed-loop approach:

**Tactile Sensors → Signal Processing → Vibrotactile Feedback → User**

Tactile/force sensors detect contact and grip at the robotic hand. The information is processed and translated into vibration patterns through an adjustable wearable band, allowing the user to perceive where and how strongly the hand is interacting with an object.

## Haptic Feedback

The tactile feedback system works through a simple closed loop:

1. **Sense** — Sensors detect contact and grip force.
2. **Interpret** — The controller processes the sensor data.
3. **Encode** — Contact and force information is converted into vibration patterns.
4. **Transmit** — Vibrotactile actuators on the wearable band provide feedback.
5. **Perceive** — The user learns to associate vibration location and intensity with touch and force.

## Tendon-Driven Actuation

The robotic hand uses tendon-based actuation to transfer motion from the actuators to the fingers.

The architecture is designed to keep the moving components lightweight while allowing flexible actuator placement and modular mechanical development.
